Introduction

The Barcelona grid network was originally designed in 1855 by renowned urban planner Ildefons Cerdà to combat issues such as overcrowding, poor ventilation, and disease outbreaks, all of which were plaguing the city (Amati et al., 2023; Castrezzati, 2023). In the 21st century, the new plague has become cars and motorized traffic, with 85% of the roads historically being dedicated to vehicles (Amati et al., 2023). As highlighted by the New York City Department of City Planning (2024), urban design needs to go beyond aesthetics and "enhance people's daily lives" by allowing people to walk comfortably whilst "confronting society's greatest challenges", which in Barcelona’s case entails improving traffic, air quality, and walkability. Barcelona’s Superblocks (Superilles) model does this by restructuring 3x3 grid cells into 10 km/h loops, reallocating over 70% of space to pedestrians (Amati et al., 2023; Anguelovski et al., 2023; Iturralde, 2021). This report aims to evaluate the Superblocks initiative by analysing its land-use and spatial efficiency, analysing the quality-of-life (QoL) split between the internal streets and the perimeter corridors, as well as assessing how environmental progress leads to property speculation and green gentrification (Anguelovski et al., 2023).
